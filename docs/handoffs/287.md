# 287 — the relay took the headset off a live call, repeatedly

Reported from real use: the user was on a call on a work laptop (no adapter
installed) and the Pixel kept claiming the headset back.

## What the log showed

Two compounding mechanisms, both confirmed from the relay log for the period.

### A — the Pixel's `media` trigger was flapping

Continuous from 15:09 to 15:36 on 2026-09-24: `media` ending and restarting
about a second later, every two to three minutes. Eight full cycles.

This kept the Pixel the rightful holder in the relay's model for the whole
period. **Filed separately as #288** — it is a defect in its own right, would
cause Mac/Pixel churn with a contending node, and is not fixed here.

Worth recording why the self-cooldown did not suppress it: `SelfCooldown` is
armed by `onClaim`/`onRelease`, and no claim or release was happening, so
nothing ever armed it. #251's 6s window never applied. This is not #251
regressing.

### B — reconciliation re-asserted the claim forever

The Pixel registered repeatedly with `observedRoutes: {"audio": false}` while
the relay held it as holder (15:40, 15:49, 15:52, 15:55, 15:58…). Each one hit
this, in `relay-service.ts`:

```ts
if (resourceState.lastHolder === node && holderBefore === node) {
  this.coalescer.dispatchImmediately(account, node, resource, "claim");
}
```

Unconditional, on every registration, every 120s. The headset was on the
laptop; the relay concluded its picture was stale and told the Pixel to take
it back. For half an hour.

**This is also #284.** The 53ms command with no holder change that produced an
audible blip was this same line firing on a periodic registration. One cause,
two symptoms.

## Why this was hard to see

Every component behaved exactly as specified. The reading was the audio route
rather than the Bluetooth link, as ADR 0018 decision 2 requires. The report was
accurate. The correction was the one the ADR describes. And the result was
thrw repeatedly taking a headset off a live call.

ADR 0018 assumed a holder without the route is a **fault to repair** — a node
that restarted. It is also what a user looks like when they move their headset
somewhere thrw cannot see, which for a locked-down work laptop is the normal
state of affairs rather than an edge case. The two are indistinguishable from
the relay, and the ADR prescribed repair unconditionally.

The ADR had already warned about this failure *shape* — under the link
reading, it says, "it would issue corrective commands forever" — and guarded
that entrance. There was a second one.

## The fix

Bounded correction, in `reassertHolderClaim`:

1. **A node already holding the route is not corrected.** Nothing to repair,
   and the command is not free — the ADR 0022 audio gate runs around every
   command, so a no-op claim still interrupts playback. This alone fixes #284.
2. **At most two corrections per holder tenure**, a tenure beginning at a
   holder *change*.
3. **The budget does not reset on the node reporting success.** This is the
   subtle part and the reason a naive retry limit would not have worked: each
   correction *succeeded* — the Pixel really did take the headset back — so a
   budget keyed on failure never depletes and the loop stays unbounded.
4. Exhaustion is logged as `reassert_exhausted`.

ADR 0018 amended accordingly, including what the change costs: a node that
genuinely loses its route more than twice in one tenure stops being repaired
until arbitration moves.

## What is deliberately not fixed

**The relay still cannot know an unmanaged device holds the resource.** Nothing
on the wire carries that. The bound stops thrw fighting the user; it does not
make thrw aware of the laptop. Designing that belongs in its own issue, not in
an amendment — #287 stays open for it.

**Mechanism A** is #288.

## Tests

`pnpm turbo lint typecheck test --filter=@thrw/relay-hosted --filter=@thrw/relay-core`
— **39 tests, 0 failures** in relay-hosted (was 37), all tasks green.

Two new, both against the real Mosquitto broker the suite already uses:

- a holder reporting `audio: true` is sent nothing further (#284);
- five registrations reporting `audio: false` produce the initial claim plus
  exactly two re-assertions, then silence (#287).

The second asserts on a settle rather than a poll, because the property is
that nothing *more* arrives, which a poll cannot express.

## Not verified on hardware

The scenario needs a third device that thrw does not manage. Reproducing it
means connecting the headset to the work laptop while the Pixel is the relay's
holder, and confirming the Pixel stops taking it back after two attempts
rather than continuing every two minutes.
